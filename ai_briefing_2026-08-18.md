## ⚖️ Policy & Regulation

- **EU AI Act transparency rules now enforceable; enforcement officially underway.** As of August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office began enforcing transparency obligations under the AI Act. AI systems must now disclose to users when they are interacting with AI rather than a human, deepfakes must be labeled, and AI-generated content must carry machine-readable watermarks. Non-compliance penalties reach 3% of global annual turnover. The enforcement of high-risk AI system provisions (credit scoring, insurance pricing, etc.) has been delayed — those rules apply from December 2027 for standalone systems and August 2028 for embedded ones. Sources: [EC Press Corner](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714) | [EU Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august)

- **China's AI agent regulations became enforceable July 15.** China's "Implementation Opinions on the Standardized Application and Innovative Development of Intelligent Agents" took effect on July 15, 2026, and represents the first national policy framework to treat AI agents as a distinct regulated category — with requirements around transparency, human oversight, and accountability for autonomous decision-making. The rules apply pressure on both domestic players and foreign companies operating in China. Source: [AI Regulation 2026 Overview](https://eveaicore.com/blog/ai-regulation-2026-what-changed)

- **US faces competing pressures: executive orders vs. state-level surge.** While the US lacks a federal AI framework comparable to the EU AI Act, a surge of state-level legislation is creating a patchwork compliance environment for AI developers. Forbes analysis from August 1 identified structural forces — liability exposure, export controls, and election integrity concerns — pushing federal regulation closer to reality, though no timeline is firm.
