## ⚖️ Policy & Regulation

- **The White House Super Intelligence Task Force formally adopted its charter and launched a 120-day AI risk review.** DNI Jay Clayton chairs the task force alongside National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross; PCAST will conduct risk evaluations covering cybersecurity, biosecurity, and model control. The 120-day timeline puts a formal report due in mid-February 2027, making this the most structured US government process for evaluating advanced AI risks to date—though critics note the mandate covers evaluation, not binding regulation. - **Massachusetts is considering some of the country's strictest AI regulations** as part of its state Senate economic development bill. The proposal would require independent evaluations of AI models and safety protocols from covered companies. The bill is still under negotiation, but follows a period of high-profile AI-driven cyberattacks and joins a growing wave of state-level AI legislation as federal action remains slow. Source: [WBUR](https://wbur.org/news/2026/10/16/ai-regulation-tech-economic-development-bill)

- **Microsoft president Brad Smith called for legally binding AI safety rules and named the UK as best-positioned to lead global regulation.** Speaking on a BBC podcast, Smith argued that voluntary safety commitments only bind already-responsible actors and proposed three concrete requirements: mandatory breach notification if AI agents are compromised, pre-release approval from independent evaluators, and kill switches to pause AI if humans lose control. His comments follow the UK AI Security Institute's access restoration and come amid calls from Dario Amodei and Sam Altman to slow AI development following rogue agent incidents.
